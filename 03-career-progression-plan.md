# 03 · Career Progression Plan (Step-by-Step)

A concrete, phased **12–18 month** plan that runs three tracks in parallel:
- 🧠 **Learn** — close technical & leadership skill gaps (details in [04](04-subjects-and-resources.md))
- 🛠️ **Operate** — start doing Staff-level work *now* (promotion is a lagging indicator)
- 📣 **Campaign** — make impact visible and activate sponsors (details in [05](05-promotion-playbook.md) & [06](06-self-assessment-and-tracking.md))

> **The governing principle:** *"Start performing like a Staff Engineer today… When you act like one consistently for 6–12 months and everyone knows how well you're performing, the promotion becomes a formality rather than a leap of faith."*[^1]

Budget: **~6 hrs/week** of deliberate study, plus applying the behaviors in your day job.

---

## Phase 0 — Foundation & Setup (Weeks 1–4)

**Goal: know exactly where you stand and set up the machinery.**

| Step | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | **Self-assess** against the jorgef radar + your company's real ladder, line by line, marking each "Achieving / Needs Work" with evidence links.[^2] | A gap list |
| 0.2 | **Pick your archetype** (Tech Lead / Architect / Solver / Right Hand) — what energizes you *and* what your company needs.[^3] | A target identity |
| 0.3 | **Start a brag document** and a **weekly "5-15" update** (15 min to write, 5 to read).[^4] | The data pipeline |
| 0.4 | **Write your (mostly empty) promotion packet** and your **career narrative** (1–5 yr goals → the 2–3 for this quarter).[^5] | The "map" |
| 0.5 | **Bring it to your manager:** *"Attaining Staff is a goal of mine. What's missing? What should I emphasize?"* Identify your sponsor(s), incl. skip-level.[^6] | An activated sponsor |

✅ **Exit criteria:** you have a ranked gap list, a named archetype, a running brag doc, and a manager who knows your goal.

---

## Phase 1 — Technical Depth + First Staff Behaviors (Months 2–6)

**Learn:** System Design Foundations → Distributed Systems (DDIA). See [04, Q1–Q2](04-subjects-and-resources.md).

**Operate — start behaving at the next level:**
- Take on **one ambiguous, cross-team problem** (move up the *ownership* axis, not just scope).[^7]
- Write **≥1 design doc / RFC** for real work; start an **ADR log**.[^8]
- **Delegate the hard parts** of your current work to grow teammates (a classic tech-lead mistake is hoarding them).[^9]

**Campaign:**
- Publish weekly updates where other teams can see them.
- Do **≥1 sponsorship/visibility action per week** (raise a peer's name, cheerlead work in Slack, forward a summary upward).[^10]

✅ **Exit criteria:** solid system-design fluency; one cross-team initiative underway; 1–2 durable docs authored.

---

## Phase 2 — Reliability, Architecture + Leading Cross-Team Work (Months 7–12)

**Learn:** SRE (SLOs, error budgets, postmortems) → Software Architecture. See [04, Q3–Q4](04-subjects-and-resources.md).

**Operate — step into technical leadership:**
- **Lead** (don't just contribute to) a multi-team technical initiative end-to-end — the raw material for a "Staff project."[^11]
- Define **SLOs** for a real service; run a **blameless postmortem** that produces a systemic fix.[^12]
- Start practicing **executive communication** (SCQA + Pyramid Principle) in your proposals; get "in the room" for a recurring decision forum.[^13]

**Campaign:**
- **Executive/skip-level visibility** — the most important kind. Build a relationship with your manager's manager (they approve Staff promotions and "rarely support folks whose work they don't know").[^14]
- Give an internal tech talk or write a company-blog post.

✅ **Exit criteria:** you're *leading* a cross-team effort with visible impact; skip-level knows your work; you're in ≥1 decision-making room.

---

## Phase 3 — Strategy, Influence + Building the Case (Months 13–18)

**Learn:** "The Hard Parts" + Security + the full **Leadership track** (StaffEng guides, *The Staff Engineer's Path*, engineering strategy/vision). See [04, Q5–Q6](04-subjects-and-resources.md).

**Operate — operate like a Staff engineer:**
- **Write an engineering strategy or vision** for a recurring problem ("five design docs, pull out the similarities").[^15]
- Be a **force multiplier** — sponsor 2–3 engineers deliberately; keep a sponsorship journal.[^10]
- Own a problem "that falls between the cracks of team ownership."[^16]

**Campaign — assemble the promotion:**
- Turn the brag doc + weekly updates into a **10–15 page portfolio / promo packet**: exec summary → 3–4 case studies (problem → approach → implementation → before/after metrics → lessons) → metrics dashboard → peer testimonials → growth trajectory.[^1][^17]
- **Peer-edit** with Staff+ colleagues, then **manager-edit asking them to "enumerate the gaps."**[^5]
- Line the packet up so it's ready *before* the calibration/promotion committee meets.[^18]

✅ **Exit criteria:** 6–12 months of demonstrable next-level work, packaged and sponsored, timed to a review cycle.

---

## The "First 90 Days Operating at the Next Level" plan

Run this as soon as you commit (it overlaps Phase 0–1). The unifying principle: *promotion recognizes work you're already doing at the next level.*[^2]

- **Days 1–30:** finish the self-assessment; write the empty promo packet; bring it to your manager; name your sponsor(s) and state your goal explicitly.[^5]
- **Days 31–60:** pick up a cross-team/ambiguous problem; start one long-lived doc; start weekly updates; make sponsorship/visibility a weekly habit.[^14]
- **Days 61–90:** land visible early impact; get skip-level exposure ("if they can't remember your work's impact in a meeting two months from now, you're unlikely to get promoted"); ask your sponsor a calibrated gap question.[^6]

## The operating cadence (run this continuously)

Full detail + templates in **[06-self-assessment-and-tracking.md](06-self-assessment-and-tracking.md)**.

| Cadence | Habits |
|---|---|
| **Weekly (~45 min)** | Write the 5-15 update · append to brag doc with impact numbers · ≥1 sponsorship/visibility action · advance your cross-team initiative one step. |
| **Monthly** | Career 1:1 with manager (review goals, ask one calibrated gap question) · ensure ≥1 long-lived doc is in flight. |
| **Quarterly** | Refresh career narrative · update promo packet + peer/manager review · pick the next gap-closing project · check the calibration calendar. |
| **Semi-annual** | Compile the 6-month brag doc; share with manager & peer reviewers before review season. |

## Milestones / checkpoints (leading indicators)

Track these in a simple spreadsheet — they make your progress *legible* and become the evidence in your packet:[^14][^19]

- **# design/architecture docs authored & reviewed**
- **# cross-team initiatives led** (toward a genuine "staff project")
- **Influence/scope trend:** Team → Multiple Teams → Company
- **# engineers mentored/sponsored** + their outcomes
- **# times raised someone's name** per week
- **Executive/skip-level touchpoints**
- **# company forums led** (architecture reviews, learning circles)
- **# internal/external talks, blog posts, OSS contributions**
- Every project logged with **quantified impact** ($, %, users, deals, audits)

> ⚠️ These are *leading indicators, not a checklist.* Track them to spot gaps and tell a narrative — never to "game" scope. Every ladder warns against reducing promotion to a checklist.[^2]

---

## Honest realities to plan around

- **Senior is the terminal level** — progress past it is the exception; be deliberate and self-directed.[^16]
- **Opportunity is unevenly distributed** — infra vs product, HQ vs remote. It's simpler to align with where opportunity is concentrated than to fight it alone.[^16]
- **~⅓ of Staff engineers got the title by switching companies.** If your org has no "oxygen" at the next level after genuine effort, be willing to move.[^16][^20]
- **It's not a pure meritocracy** — luck, timing, and sponsorship are real factors. That's *why* visibility and sponsorship are in this plan, not optional extras.[^6]
