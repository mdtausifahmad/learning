# 06 · Self-Assessment & Tracking

Run your promotion like a project. This file gives you: a **self-assessment rubric**, a **brag-doc system**, a full **operating cadence**, **measurable milestones**, and a **manager 1:1 script**.

> The whole system is one data pipeline: **weekly updates → brag doc → promo packet → scored against a ladder → calibration.** Daily work becomes a committee decision.[^1]

---

## 1. Self-assessment — score yourself

### The jorgef radar (best for a Senior→Staff self-score)
Score yourself **1–5** on each axis and plot the radar. Each level includes the ones below it.[^2]

| Axis | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Technology** | Adopts | Specializes | Evangelizes | Masters | Creates |
| **System** | Enhances | Designs | Owns | Evolves | Leads |
| **People** | Learns | Supports | Mentors | Coordinates | Manages |
| **Process** | Follows | Enforces | Challenges | Adjusts | Defines |
| **Influence** | Subsystem | Team | Multiple Teams | Company | Community |

**Your target diff (Senior D4 → Staff D5/D6):** Technology Evangelizes→Masters→Creates; System Owns→Evolves→Leads; **Influence Team→Multiple Teams→Company.** Over-index on the **Influence axis** — it's the clearest Staff signal.[^2]

### Cross-checks from other ladders
- **Dropbox** — grade yourself on **Consistency × Velocity × Accountability**, and against the five **Levers for Impact**: Domain Expertise · Innovation · Product Expertise · Project Leadership · Technical Leadership & Mentorship (you don't need all).[^3]
- **Honeycomb (PSHE)** — check *ownership*, not just scope: are you executing given work, or **discovering the problems to solve**? Staff = moving up the ownership axis.[^4]
- **CircleCI** — two dimensions: **scope** (task → epic → team → org) and **frequency** (frequently → usually → generally).[^5]
- **Aggregator:** find a ladder closest to your company among 75 public frameworks at **https://progression.fyi/**.[^6]

**How to run it:** print the jorgef radar + your company's real ladder side by side; score each 1–5; mark Senior-baseline vs Staff-target; the **deltas are your gap list.** Corroborate with 3 evidence sources: 1:1s, peer/other-team feedback, self-eval.[^2]

> ⚠️ Every ladder says it's **"not a checklist"** — use it to have meaningful career conversations and spot gaps, not to game promotion.[^2][^3]

## 2. The brag-document system

One doc per year (5–10+ pages is fine). *"You don't have to make your work sound better than it is… make it sound exactly as good as it is."*[^7]

**Sections:**[^7]
1. **Goals** (this year / next year) — share so people can support them.
2. **Projects** — for each: *your specific contributions* + *impact* (who for; numbers — $ saved, %, users; non-numeric wins like passing an audit). Go back months later and fill in what actually happened.
3. **Collaboration & mentorship** — mentoring, expert help others rely on, foundational code, high-effort reviews, questions answered, internal talks.
4. **Design & documentation** — design docs written/reviewed; docs that reduced repeated questions.
5. **Company building** — interviewing, recruiting, process/onboarding improvements.
6. **What you learned** — surfaces growth *and* gaps.
7. **Outside of work** — blog posts, talks, OSS, industry recognition.

**Include the fuzzy work** (code-quality culture, on-call, tech debt): state the goal → list actions → list even indirect effects. It's the most likely to fall under the radar.[^7]

**Share it** — with your **manager** ("this makes my job way easier… I can use it when writing your perf review"), with **peer reviewers**, and especially across **manager transitions.**[^7]

**Prompts when stuck:** *"If you were convincing a friend to join your team, what would you tell them about your work?"* · *"Did anyone tell you that you did something well recently?"*[^7]

### The feeder: weekly "5-15" updates
*"Spend fifteen minutes a week writing a report that can be read in five minutes."* Keep a new doc each week; polish for 10 minutes each Friday. At review time, compile these into your brag doc — *"inevitably I've forgotten most things I worked on, and these remind me."* Bonus: weekly notes double as a **visibility** tactic.[^8]

## 3. The full operating cadence

**WEEKLY (~30–45 min)**
- [ ] Write the **5-15 update**; post where stakeholders/other teams see it.[^8]
- [ ] **Append to the brag doc** anything shipped + impact numbers.[^7]
- [ ] Do **≥1 sponsorship/visibility action** (see §5 menu).[^9]
- [ ] Advance your **staff project / cross-team initiative** by one concrete step.[^10]

**MONTHLY**
- [ ] **Career 1:1** using your career narrative — review goals, ask one calibrated gap question.[^11]
- [ ] Ensure ≥1 **long-lived artifact** (design doc / architecture doc / RFC) is in flight.[^12]

**QUARTERLY**
- [ ] **Refresh the career narrative**; review it with your manager.[^13]
- [ ] **Update the promo packet**; peer + manager review, asking them to "enumerate the gaps."[^14]
- [ ] **Pick the next gap-closing project** aligned to a ladder gap *and* a business priority.[^13]
- [ ] Check the **calibration calendar** so a packet is ready before the committee meets.[^15]

**SEMI-ANNUAL / review season**
- [ ] Compile the **6-month brag doc** from weekly updates; run a brag workshop; share with manager & peer reviewers.[^7]

## 4. Measurable milestones / leading indicators

Track in a simple spreadsheet next to the brag doc:[^12][^10]

**Technical leadership**
- # design/architecture docs authored & reviewed
- # "staff projects" / cross-team initiatives led
- Influence/scope trend: Team → Multiple Teams → Company

**People / sponsorship**
- # engineers mentored/sponsored + their outcomes
- # times you raised someone's name this week
- # design/code reviews you led or unblocked; # cross-team questions routed

**Visibility / "times in the room"**
- Executive/skip-level touchpoints
- # company forums led; # weekly updates published; # internal blog posts / office hours

**External (optional niche-building)**
- # conference talks, podcasts, external posts, OSS contributions

**Legibility:** every project logged with **quantified impact** ($, %, users, audits). Weekly updates = the audit trail; career-narrative + packet = the rolled-up map.

## 5. Sponsorship / visibility action menu (for the weekly habit)

Do one per week — for others (and recognize what you want your own sponsor to do for you):[^9]
- Suggest someone to **lead a new project** based on demonstrated experience.
- Suggest someone as **postmortem facilitator** / visible meeting leader.
- Suggest someone **write a blog post** or **give a talk**.
- **Forward their project summary** to a wider audience, underscoring why it mattered.
- Ask a person's manager if you can **share positive feedback**.
- **Boost their work in Slack**; cite something you learned from them to influential folks.

*Definition:* sponsorship = "raising up the name of someone to help them get more opportunities to do visible, valuable work" — distinct from mentorship's advice.[^9] StaffEng's #1 visibility lever is still *"work on the things that matter"*; visibility is "a transient currency… learning and developing yourself is a permanent one" — clear the threshold, then focus on real growth.[^12]

## 6. The manager growth-plan conversation

### Step 0 — Write your Career Narrative first (don't outsource your path)
*"Take an hour and write as many goals as you can for the next one to five years. Then prioritize, pick a few for the next three to six months, and share with your manager."* Then translate goals → actions *with* your manager (they have "the superpower of finding the intersection of your interests and the business' priorities") — but participate; *"by default you'll get steered towards the contested commons."* Refresh **every quarter.**[^13]

### Step 1 — State the goal explicitly
Open with *"I'm looking to be recognized as a Staff engineer."* Be honest about what you want. Your **direct manager must be the primary sponsor**; also build a real relationship with your **skip-level.**[^11]

### Step 2 — Bring the (empty) promo packet to a 1:1
*"Review the empty packet with them, and ask what's missing, what to emphasize, and if they'd recommend adding steps."* Start it *long before* you expect promotion.[^14]

### Step 3 — Ask *calibrated* gap questions (do the answerer's work)
Avoid vague asks that produce "work on larger, high-impact projects." Instead:[^11]
- *"If I don't get promoted this cycle, what are the likely causes?"*
- *"What's the most effective thing I can do to be a stronger candidate?"*
- Best — a specific scenario: *"This quarter I completed the API refactor but the schedule slipped and frustrated PMs. How could I have handled it more effectively?"*
- Normalize *"I don't know"* so your manager doesn't invent unhelpful gaps.

### Step 4 — Support your sponsor; stay aligned
*"Ask your sponsor how you can support their sponsorship."* Don't be the person who only "visited the office the week before promotion decisions." If it truly doesn't work after ~6 months of genuine effort, consider changing teams (via the skip-level relationship).[^11]

### Ready-to-use recurring 1:1 agenda
1. Career narrative: 1–5 yr goals → the 2–3 I'm focused on this quarter.
2. Progress since last time (from brag doc / weekly updates).
3. One calibrated gap question (rotate the scenario).
4. Which upcoming project best closes a gap *and* serves a business priority?
5. Promo-packet status: what to add/emphasize before next calibration.
6. What can I do to make your sponsorship of me easier?

---

## 7. Personal goal-setting frameworks
- **"OKRs for yourself" = the Career Narrative loop** (enumerate → prioritize a few for 3–6 months → translate to actions → review quarterly).[^13]
- **OKR alignment:** map personal development goals to your org's OKRs (as CircleCI does with its matrix).[^5]
- **Deliberate practice (implicit):** "perform at the next level consistently for several months" + frequent feedback beats twice-yearly assessment.[^2]
