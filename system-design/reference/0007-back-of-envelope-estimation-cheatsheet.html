System Design · Reference 07

Back-of-the-Envelope Estimation

Compressed from Lesson 07•Print-friendly
Mindset

Estimate first and let the numbers pick the bottleneck. Round to powers of ten, keep one significant figure, state every assumption out loud, and tie each number to a design decision.

The order (always the same)

Conversions to memorise

  • 1 day ≈ 86,400 s ≈ 105 s → 1M actions/day ≈ ~10 QPS.
  • Peak ≈ 2–10× average — design for peak.
  • One commodity server: hundreds to low thousands of QPS, depending on work per request.
  • 1 Gbps ≈ 125 MB/s — networks quote bits, storage quotes bytes.
  • Real storage ≈ 3× raw (replication) plus indexes.

Memory ≈ 1000× faster than disk → cache. Sequential ≫ random → LSM-trees and Kafka append. Cross-region RTT is the speed of light → move data closer (CDN, regional replicas).

Worked numbers: URL shortener

Assume: 100M new URLs/day, read:write = 100:1, 500 B/record, keep 5 years
QPS       writes 100M / 10^5     ≈ 1.2K/s     peak (x2) ≈ 2.4K/s
          reads  x100            ≈ 120K/s     peak (x2) ≈ 240K/s
Storage   100M x 365 x 5         ≈ 180B rows  x 500 B ≈ 90 TB raw, ~270 TB at 3x
Bandwidth reads 120K/s x 500 B   ≈ 60 MB/s    (writes ≈ 600 KB/s)
Cache     10B reads/day x 500 B  ≈ 5 TB/day   hot 20% ≈ 1 TB
Codes     base-62, 7 chars       ≈ 3.5 trillion combinations

Decisions the numbers force: read-dominated → cache + replicas/CDN first; ~270 TB → shard by hash of the short code; 240K peak QPS → many stateless app servers behind a load balancer.

Review questions

  • What are the stated assumptions (DAU, ratio, payload, retention)?
  • Are average and peak, read and write, computed separately?
  • Does storage include replication and indexes?
  • Is bandwidth in bytes or bits — consistently?
  • Which number justifies each cache, replica and shard in the design?
Traps
  • False precision — “1,157.4 QPS” instead of “~1K/s”.
  • Forgetting peak — sized for average, down at the daily spike.
  • Ignoring replication and overhead — real storage is ~3× raw.
  • Mixing bits and bytes — an 8× error.
  • Numbers not tied to decisions — estimation as ritual, not design.
