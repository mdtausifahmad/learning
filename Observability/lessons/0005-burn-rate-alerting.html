Reliability & SRE · Lesson 05

Burn-Rate Alerting on SLOs

◷ ~12 min read • Level: Foundations → Judgment • Prereq: L03 budget · Ref 03 recording rules

You freeze at zero budget (Lesson 04) — but you want to know you’re in trouble before zero, and be woken only when it’s real. Burn-rate alerting pages you on how fast you’re spending the budget. This is where your recording rules finally page a human — and where you kill the alert noise that makes on-call miserable.

1. What a good alert optimises

Judge any alerting scheme on four attributes:

AttributeMeaning
PrecisionOf the alerts that fired, what fraction were significant? (Low precision = noise.)
RecallOf the significant events, what fraction alerted? (Low recall = missed outages.)
Detection timeHow long until it fires. (Slow = more budget burned first.)
Reset timeHow long it keeps firing after the problem is fixed. (Long = ignored alerts.)

A “significant” event is one that consumes a large fraction of your error budget. That’s the whole trick: tie the page to the budget, not to raw errors.

2. Why the obvious approaches fail

Naive approachWhy it breaks
Alert if error rate over 10 min ≥ SLOFires on tiny insignificant blips — terrible precision, pager fatigue.
Widen the window to 36 h (5% budget)Precise, but a 2-minute outage keeps the alert firing for 36 hours. Awful reset.
Add a for: durationDoesn’t scale with severity, and resets on any momentary recovery — spiky outages can burn 35% of budget and never alert.

Static thresholds and durations can’t be precise, sensitive, and reset quickly at the same time. You need a different quantity.

3. The unlock: alert on burn rate

Burn rate, defined

Burn rate is how fast, relative to the SLO, you’re consuming the error budget. A burn rate of 1 spends the budget exactly evenly — empty precisely at the window’s end. (99.9% over 30 days ↔ a steady 0.1% error rate.) A burn rate of N empties the budget in 1/N of the window.

Burn rateTime to exhaust a 30-day budget
1×30 days
6×5 days
14.4×~50 hours
36×~20 hours
1000×~43 minutes

Now the alert threshold is burn rate × (1 − SLO), and the budget consumed when it fires is just burn rate × (window ÷ period). Pick the budget-spend you care about, and the burn rate falls out. 2% of a 30-day budget in 1 hour = a burn rate of 14.4.

4. The recipe: multi-burn-rate, multi-window

Two refinements turn burn-rate alerts into the production-grade answer:

  • Multiple burn rates — a fast page for catastrophes and a slow ticket for leaks → good recall across severities.
  • Multiple windows — pair each long window (significance) with a short window = 1/12 its length, and fire only when both exceed the threshold. The short window confirms you’re still burning → the alert resets minutes after the problem ends, not hours.

The Workbook’s recommended starting parameters (99.9% SLO, 30-day window):

SeverityLong windowShort windowBurn rateBudget in window
Page1 hour5 min14.4×2%
Page6 hours30 min6×5%
Ticket3 days6 hours1×10%

5. In your stack (the payoff)

These alerts just read the ratio_rate series your recording rules already produce (Reference 03) — that’s why you recorded several windows. With 0.001 = 1 − 99.9%: 

# PAGE — fast burn (fires only if BOTH windows exceed the threshold)
- alert: ErrorBudgetBurn_Page
  expr: |
    (
      job:slo_error:ratio_rate1h  > (14.4 * 0.001)
      and job:slo_error:ratio_rate5m  > (14.4 * 0.001)
    )
    or
    (
      job:slo_error:ratio_rate6h  > (6 * 0.001)
      and job:slo_error:ratio_rate30m > (6 * 0.001)
    )
  labels: { severity: page }

# TICKET — slow leak
- alert: ErrorBudgetBurn_Ticket
  expr: |
    job:slo_error:ratio_rate3d > (1 * 0.001)
    and job:slo_error:ratio_rate6h > (1 * 0.001)
  labels: { severity: ticket }
The low-traffic trap

This recipe assumes enough traffic to be a meaningful signal. At 10 requests/hour, a single failure is a 10% error rate — a 1000× burn that pages instantly, allowing only ~7 failures per 30 days. For low-QPS services: generate synthetic traffic, aggregate related services into one SLO, or accept a coarser target. Don’t blind-copy the numbers onto a sleepy service.

Try it in your own system
  • Take your SLO’s 1 − SLO and your recording rules (Reference 03).
  • Write the page alert: 14.4× over 1h+5m, OR 6× over 6h+30m.
  • Write the ticket alert: 1× over 3d+6h.
  • Sanity-check: is your request rate high enough for a single failure not to page?

Bring it — and we’ll have closed the full SLO loop: measure → target → budget → policy → alert. Next we widen the lens to observability.
