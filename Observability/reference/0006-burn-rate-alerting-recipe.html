Burn-Rate Alerting — Recipe

Compressed from Lesson 05•Print-friendly

Judge every alert on four attributes

  • Precision — fraction of alerts that were significant (low = noise).
  • Recall — fraction of significant events caught (low = missed outages).
  • Detection time — how fast it fires.
  • Reset time — how fast it stops firing once fixed.

Burn rate

Definition

How fast, relative to the SLO, you spend the budget. Rate 1 empties it exactly at the window’s end; rate N empties it in 1/N of the window.

alert threshold        = burn rate × (1 − SLO)
budget consumed @ fire = burn rate × (window ÷ period)
BurnEmpties 30-day budget in
1×30 days
6×5 days
14.4×~50 hours
36×~20 hours
1000×~43 minutes

The recommended config (99.9% SLO / 30-day)

SeverityLongShort (=long/12)BurnBudget
Page1 hour5 min14.4×2%
Page6 hours30 min6×5%
Ticket3 days6 hours1×10%
  • Multi-burn-rate (fast page + slow ticket) → recall across severities.
  • Multi-window: fire only if both long & short exceed threshold → fast reset, fewer false positives.

PromQL (reads Reference 03 recording rules)

# PAGE
(rate1h > 14.4*0.001 and rate5m  > 14.4*0.001)
or
(rate6h > 6*0.001    and rate30m > 6*0.001)

# TICKET
rate3d > 1*0.001 and rate6h > 1*0.001

# 0.001 = 1 − 99.9% SLO. Swap in your own (1 − SLO).

Avoid these

  • Alert on raw error rate ≥ SLO over 10 min → noisy, low precision.
  • One giant window (36 h) → a 2-min outage fires for 36 h (awful reset).
  • for: durations → don’t scale with severity; spiky outages slip through.
Low-traffic trap

At 10 req/h, one failure = 10% = 1000× burn = instant page (only ~7 failures / 30 days allowed). Fixes: synthetic traffic, aggregate services into one SLO, or a coarser target.
