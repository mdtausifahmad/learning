A one-page, pre-agreed rule for what the org does as the budget drains. Modelled on Google’s example policy. Replace the italic prompts and get it signed.

Error-Budget Policy — Service name

Owner: name · Approver: senior exec · Approved: date · Revisit: date +12mo

Scope. Applies to which releases / components.

Goals. Protect users from repeated SLO misses; give an incentive to balance reliability with features. This policy is not a punishment — it gives the team permission to focus on reliability when the data says it matters more than the next feature.

SLO(s). e.g. 99.9% availability; 99% of requests < 300 ms, over a 4-week rolling window.

When at or above SLO. Releases proceed per the normal release process.

When the budget is exhausted. Halt all changes and releases except P0 incidents and security fixes until the service is back within SLO.

Cause matters:

Must stop & fix reliability if…May keep shipping if…
your bug / procedural error burned the budgeta company-wide network problem did
a postmortem finds a hard dependency to softenanother team’s service failed & they froze
mis-categorised errors hid a real SLO missout-of-scope users burned it (load tests, pentests)

Triggers. A single incident burning >20% of the 4-week budget → mandatory postmortem with ≥1 P0 action item. A class of outage >20% per quarter → P0 on quarterly planning.

Escalation. Disagreements on budget calculation or required actions escalate to named executive for a decision.

Signatures. Product ___ · Dev lead ___ · Ops/SRE ___ · Approver ___

Requirements checklist

  • Written & specific — concrete actions, not “be careful”.
  • Approved by a senior owner with authority to enforce the freeze.
  • Escalation path defined — up, not sideways.
  • Exceptions named (P0/security, out-of-scope traffic) — sparingly.
  • Revisit date set — it’s a living agreement.
Why it works

Changes cause ~70% of outages. The budget is the control that diverts attention to stability exactly when data demands it — letting you ship fast and stay reliable, instead of trading them off by gut feel.
