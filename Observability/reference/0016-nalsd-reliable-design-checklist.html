NALSD & the Reliable-Design Checklist

Compressed from Lesson 15 (capstone)•Print-friendly
Non-abstract

Turn the whiteboard into concrete resource estimates — QPS, bytes/day, machines, bandwidth. Reliability is the top feature. Sound assumptions beat perfect numbers.

The four questions (iterate)

PhaseQuestion
Basic designPossible? — ignoring limits, does it satisfy the requirements?
Better? — faster / smaller / more efficient?
Scale upFeasible? — within money/hardware; what distributed design?
Resilient? — component & whole-datacenter failure?

Back-of-envelope template

1. Requirements AS SLOs        e.g. 99.9% < 1s ; data < 5 min old
2. Load                        QPS, writes/s, users
3. Data                        QPS × bytes/entry  →  MB/s  →  GB–TB/day
4. Machines                    load / per-machine capacity  (+ overhead)
5. Redundancy                  N+2 ; replicate across zones/regions
6. Bottleneck?                 RAM? CPU? bandwidth? → reshape (shard, cache, pre-aggregate)

The completeness test (a design isn’t done until…)

QuestionCovered by
Measured? SLOs, golden signals, observabilityLessons 1–3, 6, 7
Fails & degrades? overload, graceful degradationLessons 11, 12
Recovers? incident response, rollbackLessons 9, 13
Changes safely? error-budget policy, canary/flagsLessons 4, 13
Owned & simple? clear owner, explainable at 3amLessons 9, 14
Pays rent?

Every component: removes more complexity than it adds? reduces blast radius? has an owner? explainable at 03:00? If not, cut it.
