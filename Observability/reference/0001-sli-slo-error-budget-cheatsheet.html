SLI · SLO · Error Budget — Cheat Sheet

Compressed from Lesson 01•Print-friendly

The four words, tight

  • SLI — a measured signal of user experience, as a ratio: good events ÷ valid events. (e.g. requests < 300 ms without error.)
  • SLO — the target for an SLI over a window. (e.g. 99.9% over 30 days.)
  • SLA — an SLO with a contractual consequence (refund, penalty).
  • Error budget — the allowed unreliability: 1 − SLO. A currency you spend on change.
The five-second SLA test

“What happens if it’s missed?”   No consequence ⇒ it’s an SLO.   A refund/penalty ⇒ it’s an SLA.

The one reframe

100% is the wrong target. Each extra nine costs ~10× more while users — on their own flaky devices — can’t tell. Aim for reliable enough; treat the SLO as floor and ceiling. The gap to 100% is your error budget — spend it.

“Downtime” here means the equivalent budget of failed requests, not necessarily a full outage. For a request-based SLI, spend it however it accrues.

A good SLO is…

  • Tied to a user journey (checkout, login, search).
  • Few in number and understandable by non-experts.
  • A ratio of good/valid events, from the user’s side.
  • Achievable under normal conditions, agreed by stakeholders.

A bad SLO is…

  • On host CPU / uptime — green while users suffer.
  • So strict teams ignore it, or so loose users feel pain first.
  • One of fifty — nobody knows which one matters.
  • A dashboard number with no policy attached.

Error budget as policy

Budget healthy → ship fast, take risks
Budget spent → freeze & stabilise

The budget arbitrates “feature vs. stability” so it stops being political. Both product and engineering read the same number. Without an SLO, there is no SRE — just heroics.

Maturity ladder — find yourself

0Greenfield — nothing deployed.
1Monitoring, but no SLO; unspoken 100% goal. ← most teams start here.
2An SLO exists, but with no teeth.
3SLO + error-budget policy drives decisions. ← the goal.
Anti-pattern

“Let’s aim for 100% uptime.” It’s impossible, unaffordable, and invisible to users. It also kills velocity and makes every blip feel like failure. Choose a target below 100% on purpose — and spend the budget.
