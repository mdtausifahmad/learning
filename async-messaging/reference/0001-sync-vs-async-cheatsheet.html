Async & Messaging · Reference 01

Sync ↔ Async — Decision Cheat Sheet

Compressed from Lesson 01•Print-friendly

The one-line test

Decide in five seconds

Does the caller need the answer to take its next step?
Yes → synchronous.   No (soon, not now) → asynchronous.

Reach for SYNCHRONOUS when…

  • The result gates the next line of code (auth, payment auth, validation).
  • You need a value back right now to respond to a user.
  • Strong, read-your-writes consistency is required immediately.
  • The interaction is simple and the callee is reliable and fast.

Reach for ASYNCHRONOUS when…

  • Work must happen soon, but the caller shouldn’t wait (email, indexing).
  • One event should fan out to many independent consumers.
  • You must absorb traffic spikes (load leveling) or survive downstream outages.
  • You want to decouple teams/services so they deploy and scale independently.

The structural move

Replace a direct call with: Producer → Broker (durable buffer) → Consumer. The broker holds the message until the consumer is ready. This decouples the two in time — the heart of everything.

Benefits ↔ Costs (say both out loud)

You gainYou pay
Loose runtime coupling (add consumers freely)A broker to run and keep highly available
Availability — outages degrade, not cascadeEventual results, not immediate
Load leveling — spikes become queue depthRequest/reply becomes harder (no return value)
Many styles: pub/sub, notify, async replyNew failure modes: duplicates, reorder, delay

Vocabulary anchors

  • Synchronous — caller sends and waits, holding its thread, for the reply.
  • Asynchronous — caller hands off a message and continues immediately.
  • Temporal coupling — sender and receiver must be healthy at the same instant. Async removes it.
  • Message — a self-contained packet of data passed between components via the broker.
  • Broker — infrastructure that durably stores and delivers messages (Kafka, RabbitMQ, SQS…).
Anti-pattern

“Let’s just put a queue in front of it.” A queue in front of a call that needs an immediate answer doesn’t remove the wait — it hides it and adds failure modes. Apply the one-line test first.
