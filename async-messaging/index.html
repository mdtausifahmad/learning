Principal-Engineering Track

Async Messaging: Level 0 → Staff

19 lessons · 19 cheat sheets · 1 capstone · 1 full Markdown guide•Kotlin · Spring Boot · Kafka · PostgreSQL · Podman

From why async exists to running Kafka exactly-once, sagas and PostgreSQL CDC in production — the judgement a staff engineer needs. Each lesson is a single sitting, with step-through animations, quizzes and a cheat sheet. Prefer one long read? The whole course is also in GUIDE.md (with Mermaid diagrams). See MISSION.md for the why.

Nineteen interactive lessons take you from temporal coupling and queues vs topics to Kafka consumer-group internals, delivery guarantees, idempotency, ordering, backpressure and dead-letter queues. They then cover the architecture layer — log vs queue brokers, choreography vs orchestration, the transactional outbox, sagas, schema evolution, event sourcing and CQRS — and finish with observability and an animated atlas of PostgreSQL CDC with Debezium. Examples use Kotlin and Spring Boot with Kafka and PostgreSQL, and a hands-on order-saga capstone ties it together.

Part I · Foundations

Why async exists, queues vs topics, Kafka consumer groups, and naming intent.

  1. Why Async? The Problem Messaging Solves
    Temporal coupling, the four cracks of sync calls, and the durable buffer that fixes them.
  2. Queues vs Topics — One Consumer, or Many?
    Point-to-point vs publish-subscribe, competing consumers, and how Kafka’s groupId decides.
  3. Why Consumers ≤ Partitions — Kafka’s Consumer Group Machinery
    Group protocol, eager vs cooperative rebalances, static membership, assignors, adding partitions.
  4. Event vs Command vs Query — Naming What Flows
    Facts, requests and questions: naming messages by intent and who owns the decision.

Part II · Delivery semantics & Kafka exactly-once

What a crash does to your guarantees, and how Kafka EOS works and is operated.

  1. Delivery Guarantees — At-Most, At-Least, Exactly-Once
    Commit order decides loss vs duplicates; two routes to effectively-once processing.
  2. Inside Kafka Exactly-Once — Sequence Numbers & read_committed
    Idempotent producer, transactions, zombie fencing, markers, the LSO and read_committed.
  3. Operating Kafka Exactly-Once — Lag, transactional.id & the Commit Dial
    Hung transactions that look like lag, choosing transactional.id, the commit-interval trade-off.

Part III · Reliability patterns

Idempotency, ordering, backpressure and retries/DLQs — the everyday toolkit.

  1. Idempotency & Deduplication in Depth
    Natural idempotency, idempotency keys, dedup stores and windows, broker-native dedup.
  2. Ordering & Partitioning
    Per-key order, choosing the partition key, what breaks ordering, per-broker ordering models.
  3. Backpressure & Load Leveling
    Spikes vs sustained overload, pull vs push, prefetch, lag-based autoscaling, load shedding.
  4. Retries, Dead-Letter Queues & Poison Messages
    Transient vs permanent failures, bounded backoff with jitter, DLQs as an owned workflow.

Part IV · Broker models & distributed workflows

Log vs queue, choreography vs orchestration, the outbox and sagas.

  1. Log vs Queue Brokers — the One Difference That Explains Everything
    Consume-and-delete vs append-and-retain: replay, fan-out, ordering, DLQ and when to pick each.
  2. Event-Driven Architecture — Choreography vs Orchestration
    Events vs commands for multi-service flows, the visibility trap, hybrids and workflow engines.
  3. The Transactional Outbox & the Dual-Write Problem
    Lost and phantom events, the outbox table, polling relay vs CDC, at-least-once publishing.
  4. The Saga Pattern & Distributed Workflows
    Local transactions plus compensations, semantic locks, pivot ordering, missing isolation.

Part V · Evolution, history & operations

Schema contracts, event sourcing and CQRS, observability and when not to.

  1. Schema Evolution & Contracts
    Backward/forward/full compatibility, Avro vs Protobuf vs JSON, schema registry, migrations.
  2. Event Sourcing & CQRS
    Events as the source of truth, folds and projections, CQRS read models, and when not to.
  3. Observability, Operations & When Not to Use Messaging
    Trace context across hops, the key metrics, an incident runbook, and when to skip the broker.

Part VI · CDC with PostgreSQL & Debezium

An animated atlas of logical decoding, slots, snapshots and change events.

  1. PostgreSQL CDC with Debezium — an Animated Atlas
    WAL, logical decoding, slots, snapshots, change events and slot operations, end to end.
