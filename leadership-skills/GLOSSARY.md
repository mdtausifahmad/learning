# Leadership Skills — Glossary

The canonical language for this workspace. Lessons and reference sheets use these terms exactly.

## Influence

**Borrowed authority**:
The Staff+ form of authority. It doesn't come from command; it is borrowed from expertise, trust, context, relationships and executive sponsorship, and has to be renewed continually. Paradoxically, it shrinks as scope grows.
_Avoid_: Positional power, "my call" (you rarely control roadmaps, people or rituals)

**Learn to never be wrong**:
Building judgement, preparation and communication habits that make your contributions reliably useful. You are right in a Staff+ way when the group gets closer to the truth *and* still feels it owns the answer. It does not mean pretending to be infallible.
_Avoid_: Sucking the oxygen out of the room (answering first, correcting every imprecision, winning by exhaustion)

**The three moves**:
What to do before you assert anything: **ask good questions** (three before sharing your view), **define the purpose** (decision, brainstorm, risk review or status), and **read the room** (converge if close; subgroup, gather data or escalate with options if far apart).

**To lead, you have to follow**:
Influence is finite. Ask "will this matter to me in six months?" If not, actively support a trustworthy leader's direction so the organisation moves faster. Your rare objections then carry weight.
_Avoid_: Passivity (following is a deliberate choice)

**Non-blocking feedback**:
Feedback that is explicitly labelled as not delaying the decision, e.g. "Optional nit…" or "Non-blocking concern…". The tone must match the label.

**Escalation ladder**:
The order for handling difficult behaviour: private curiosity → shared framing → meeting boundary → structured decision → manager or sponsor alignment.

**Passive vs active influence**:
Passive influence is being a role model; people copy how high-status engineers behave. Active influence is deliberately shaping the organisation through coalitions, strategy, pre-meetings and decision systems. Staff+ engineers use both and watch the shadow they cast.

## Communication

**SCQA**:
Situation, Complication, Question, Answer. A four-part executive opening that creates narrative tension without burying the conclusion.

**Minto Pyramid**:
Lead with the answer, then group supporting arguments logically: at most three top arguments with at most three sub-points each, ordered by importance rather than chronology.
_Avoid_: Academic presentation (building suspense toward the conclusion)

**Nemawashi**:
Preparing the ground before a formal decision. You share drafts early with the decision-maker, veto holders and affected teams, so the meeting confirms the path rather than discovers it.
_Avoid_: Manipulation (nemawashi makes intent legible; it doesn't hide it)

**Executive memo**:
A one-page decision document: title, ask, recommendation, why now, evidence, options, risks, decision record and appendix. It leads with the answer and names the ask (decision, advice, escalation, funding or awareness).

**Informing executives = absolution**:
Surprises are worse than bad news. Surfacing a risk early gives leaders options; hiding it hands them the problem with none.

## Project leadership

**Technical direction (as product management)**:
Acting as a part-time product manager for the engineering system. You start from the organisation's needs, users and constraints, and choose technology as a means.
_Avoid_: Technology-centred leadership (a favourite framework in search of a problem)

**Aggressive delegation**:
Deliberately giving hard, growth-producing work to others, with scaffolding: outcome, authority, guardrails, support and visibility. It is not dumping leftovers. It moves you from **essential** (the hardest work waits on you) to **adjacent** (close enough to guide, unblock and protect quality, without being a bottleneck).

**Shadow org chart**:
How work actually moves: trusted reviewers, incident veterans, informal architects, managers with veto, and people whose silence signals risk. Use it alongside the formal reporting chart, not instead of it.

**Technical quality program**:
Managing quality as an escalating system: hot spots → best practices → leverage points → technical vectors → measurement → quality team → funded program. Start with the cheapest useful tool. Frame by consequence, never by morality.

**Landed**:
A project is done when the intended behaviour has changed, the system is operating safely, owners know what remains, and the organisation has learned something durable. Merged code is not enough.

## Sponsorship

**Mentorship**:
Giving advice and feedback, usually in private, with time and experience as the currency. Done well, it has a goal, a cadence and an exit, and it builds autonomy rather than dependence.

**Sponsorship**:
Spending your credibility to create opportunity, access, visibility and promotion evidence for someone else, usually in public or in the room where decisions are made. Anyone with credibility can sponsor; you don't need to be a manager.
_Avoid_: Mentorship (advice is not access)

**Over-mentored, under-sponsored**:
A recurring pattern, especially for women and non-binary engineers: lots of advice, but few visible stretch opportunities and few senior advocates in decision rooms. This produces uneven promotion evidence even when talent is even.

**Create space**:
When meaningful work lands near you, ask "who could grow from this?", scaffold their success, then let it stay theirs. Don't take the microphone back at the moment of success.

**Sponsorship journal**:
A running log of person, opportunity, action taken, evidence or feedback sent, and follow-up date. It stops sponsorship being limited to whoever is top of mind.

## Trust & capital

**Sponsor (your)**:
The person, usually your manager, whose delegated trust gives you proxied authority. You serve at their pleasure: they must be able to predict your moves, defend your judgement and explain your work.

**Never surprise your manager**:
Keep the information flow early and regular so your sponsor can act and defend you. Treat every surprise as an incident. The weekly sponsor update covers outcomes, risks, decisions needed, **stakeholder temperature** (who is aligned, concerned or needs a pre-read) and next moves. The reciprocal rule: don't let your sponsor surprise you.

**Managing up**:
Making your manager more effective. Know how they are measured, which promises they have made upward, and what evidence helps them advocate for engineering. Bring options, not raw problems.

**Political capital**:
Trust treated as a bank account. Deposits are reliability, context, generosity and good judgement. Withdrawals are asks, escalations and requests to accept a trade-off. Only withdrawing makes you expensive; only depositing makes you liked but ineffective.

**Peer network**:
Relationships with Staff+ peers, Product/Design leads, Ops/Support/Security/Legal, and external communities. Build it before you need it, because the work gets lonelier as scope grows.

## Glue work

**Glue work**:
The coordination, care, alignment and question-asking that holds a team together but often isn't counted as engineering output. Examples: onboarding, roadmap upkeep, clarifying questions, picking up dropped balls. Done deliberately, it is leadership. Left unconscious, it limits careers.

**Promotable vs non-promotable work**:
Promotable work is what decision-makers associate with the next level. It changes how a team works, reduces future coordination cost, leaves durable artifacts, develops people and is visible to the right stakeholders. Non-promotable work is necessary but rarely rewarded, e.g. *always* the note-taker or scheduler.

**Default sink**:
The person (often junior or underrepresented) whom invisible work flows to by default because they are reliable and nobody else wants it. Staff+ engineers rotate the work and name it in planning.

**Brag doc**:
A running memory system for outcomes: problem, action, before and after, beneficiaries, artifacts and feedback. It is the raw material for the promotion packet (see `../06-self-assessment-and-tracking.md`).

## Decisions

**Transferring judgement**:
The real Staff+ decision work: explain your reasoning, show trade-offs, delegate reversible calls and leave reusable artifacts, so teams decide well when you are not in the room.

**Decision record**:
A lightweight written decision: context, goals and non-goals, options, trade-offs, recommendation with confidence, and a **revisit trigger**: the pre-agreed evidence or date that would change course, set before emotions attach to the plan. It teaches reasoning rather than announcing an answer.

**Style vs substance**:
Substance feedback changes correctness, risk, strategy, cost or learning; give it. Style feedback changes taste, naming or acceptable alternatives; usually skip it or label it non-blocking. Test: "Would changing this affect the outcome?"

**DACI / RACI**:
Role frameworks. **DACI** (Driver, Approver, Contributors, Informed) is for cross-functional decisions; don't confuse the approver with consensus. **RACI** (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) is for operational ownership; watch for too many consulted.

**One-way / two-way door**:
A one-way door is an irreversible or costly decision and deserves care. A two-way door is a reversible decision: learn by trying, and don't over-document. Don't mistake fear for irreversibility.

**Disagree and commit**:
After real debate, support execution of a decision you argued against. It only works if dissent was safe first; otherwise commitment becomes compliance.
_Avoid_: Using it to shut down debate early

**Resulting**:
Judging a decision only by its outcome (Annie Duke, *Thinking in Bets*). Good decisions can have bad outcomes and bad decisions can get lucky, so evaluate evidence, alternatives, assumptions and revisit triggers separately.

**Calibrated question**:
An open "what/how" question that invites the other side to solve the problem with you, e.g. "What would make this unsafe?" or "What am I missing?" (from *Never Split the Difference*).

## Prioritization

**Leverage**:
Value created per unit of scarce attention. At Staff+ it comes mostly from what you choose *not* to do.

**Snacking / preening / chasing ghosts**:
The three anti-patterns from *Work on what matters*. **Snacking** is easy, low-impact work that feels productive. **Preening** is high-visibility, low-impact work. **Chasing ghosts** is high-effort work on vague problems the organisation won't value.

**Existential issue**:
A problem with a large downside (customer trust, reliability, security, strategy or talent concentration) that the organisation is underestimating and that Staff+ attention can move. It comes first, and it is often quiet rather than loud.

**Matter, room, attention**:
After existential issues, invest where the work matters, there is room for meaningful improvement, and the organisation has enough attention or sponsorship to act.

**Future You**:
Your future self, treated as a stakeholder in your current calendar. Future You wishes you had written the decision down, built the dashboard before the incident and trained someone before you went on leave. Serve Future You by **telegraphing**: make future constraints (the forming risk, the needed investment, the decision window) visible early, before urgency narrows the options.

**Forty-year career**:
Will Larson's reminder that sustainable pacing beats heroics. Choose fewer, better bets, alternate intensity with recovery, and build systems instead of dependency.
