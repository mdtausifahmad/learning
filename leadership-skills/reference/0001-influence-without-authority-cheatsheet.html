Influence Without Authority

Compressed from Lesson 01•Print-friendly
Mindset

Your authority is borrowed. You make the system want to move. Being right in a Staff+ way means the group gets closer to the truth and still feels it owns the answer.

Where borrowed authority comes from

SourceEarned byLost by
ExpertiseClear reasoning, accurate judgement, useful patternsOverstated certainty, being out of date
TrustKept commitments, fairness, protecting peopleSurprises, blame, hidden agendas
ContextKnowing product, operations and org constraintsIgnoring realities outside your specialty
RelationshipsHelping people succeed before you need themOnly turning up when you want something
Exec sponsorshipAligning with business prioritiesUsing escalation instead of influence

The three moves (before you assert anything)

  • Ask three good questions — surface constraints, values and assumptions before giving your view.
  • Name the purpose — decision, brainstorm, risk review or status? Use it sparingly, or you become a process tax.
  • Read the room — if positions are close, summarise and converge. If far apart, split a subgroup, gather data, or escalate with options.

Questions that create movement

  • “What outcome are we optimising for: latency, operability, cost, or time to learn?”
  • “What would make this proposal unacceptable?”
  • “Who has to live with this decision after launch?”
  • “What evidence would change our minds?”

Rewrite table: precise, generous, changeable

Instead of…Say…
“That will not work.”“I see one risk: migration ordering. How are we thinking about that?”
“We already tried this.”“We tried something similar in billing; the failure mode was load shape. Is this context different?”
“The right answer is X.”“My current recommendation is X because of A and B. I’d change it if C is true.”
“You are missing the point.”“Can we separate the reliability goal from the implementation choice?”

Difficult people: work before the meeting

  • Pre-meet — ask what they are worried about; let them shape the agenda.
  • Find the real risk — many “blockers” are protecting a legitimate risk badly.
  • Invite a respected ally — someone they won’t be a jerk to (manager, peer, product lead), so professional behaviour is the easy path.
  • Escalation ladder — private curiosity → shared framing → meeting boundary → structured decision → manager or sponsor alignment.

Follow or fight? (to lead, you have to follow)

SituationMove
Won’t matter in six monthsFollow quickly; mark feedback optional, or stay quiet
Matters, and the leader is trustworthyBack the direction visibly; offer targeted help
Matters, and you don’t yet trust the planAsk clarifying questions; surface risks
Risk is not existentialDisagree and commit
Risk is existentialEscalate with options, not complaints

Non-blocking phrases

  • “Optional nit: this name may age poorly.”
  • “Non-blocking concern: consider an escape hatch for rollout.”
  • “I support the direction; this comment should not delay the decision.”
  • “I am aligned. If helpful, I can review the migration plan.”

Passive vs active influence

ModeSounds likeUse forRisk
Passive (role model)“Here is how I approach incidents.”Norms, craft, cultureUnintended signals: speculation read as direction
Active (org shaping)“I want us to adopt this migration pattern.”Direction, alignment, prioritiesOverreach, fatigue

One-page influence plan

  • Change — what decision or behaviour needs to change?
  • Owner — who owns the decision? (Don’t influence the wrong room.)
  • Impacted — who lives with it? Who holds a veto?
  • Incentives — what does each party care about? Turn arguments into trade-offs.
  • Proof — what evidence ends the opinion loop?
  • Fallback — what will you do if it stalls? This keeps escalation unemotional.
Traps
  • Confusing being right with being effective, or winning by exhaustion.
  • Sucking the oxygen out of the room: answering first and correcting every imprecision.
  • Escalating to executives before doing peer alignment.
  • Reframing every discussion until you feel like churn.
  • “Optional” feedback delivered in a mandatory tone.
  • Spending influence on small preferences, leaving none for existential issues.
