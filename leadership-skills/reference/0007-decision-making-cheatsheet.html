Leadership Skills · Reference 07

Decision-Making

Compressed from Lesson 07•Print-friendly
Mindset

At Staff+ scale the job is transferring judgement, not making every call. The best evidence of your leadership is a good decision made when you weren’t in the room. Be direct about the issue and curious about the person.

Transfer judgement

  • Explain reasoning — how you think, not just what you decided.
  • Show trade-offs — and the constraints behind them.
  • Delegate reversible calls — let others make them.
  • Review decision quality — after the fact, without blame.
  • Leave reusable artifacts — that future teams can copy.

Lightweight decision record

SectionPurpose
ContextWhy this decision exists now
Goals / non-goalsStop accidental scope expansion
OptionsReal alternatives, including the rejected ones
Trade-offsMake the values explicit
RecommendationThe call, plus your confidence
Revisit triggerThe evidence that would change course

Circulate early: three passes

  • Framing — “Are we solving the right problem?”
  • Options — “What alternatives or constraints am I missing?”
  • Review — “Can you live with this recommendation?”

Style vs substance: should I comment?

TypeExampleGive it?
Correctness“This migration can lose writes during failover.”Yes
Risk“This assumes Security can review in one day.”Yes
Strategy“Faster locally, but more platform fragmentation.”Yes
Preference“I’d have used a different library.”Usually no
Style“I dislike this diagram layout.”Only if it blocks understanding

Test: Would changing this affect the outcome? Labels: blocking · risk · question · non-blocking preference · “Nit, ignore if not useful.”

Should I weigh in at all?

Context others lack? · Irreversible or high blast radius? · A principle to teach? · A conflict to unblock? · Would silence create risk, or would involvement create dependence? Attention is an intervention.

Pick the lightest framework

FrameworkBest forKey questionWatch out for
DACICross-functional decisionsWho drives, approves, contributes, is informed?Approver ≠ consensus
RACIOperational ownershipResponsible, accountable, consulted, informed?Too many consulted
One-way doorIrreversible or costlyCan we undo this cheaply?Treating fear as irreversibility
Two-way doorReversibleCan we learn by trying?Over-documenting low-risk calls
Disagree & commitAfter real debateCan I support execution anyway?Using it to silence dissent early

Decision quality ≠ outcome quality

Good outcomeBad outcome
Good decisionReinforce the principle; check for luckLearn from uncertainty; don’t blame the process unfairly
Bad decisionDon’t let luck become precedentFix reasoning, roles, evidence paths

Judge the decision: best available evidence? base rates? assumptions named? real alternatives? process weight matched to risk? revisit triggers set? Judging only by outcome is resulting.

Uncertainty template (script)

I am 70% confident in option B because it reduces migration risk and keeps customer commitments. The biggest uncertainty is support load. If tickets exceed X per week after beta, we pause rollout and revisit option C.

  • State confidence — a number, not an adjective.
  • Base rates + leading indicators — from similar projects.
  • Prefer reversible experiments.
  • Revisit date — set before emotions attach to the plan.
  • Premortem — “Six months on, this failed. What happened?”

Productive disagreement

Calibrated questions

  • “What problem are you solving?”
  • “How would we know this works?”
  • “What would make this unsafe?”
  • “What am I missing?”

Change your mind visibly

“The operational data changes my view.” · “I optimised for build cost and missed support cost.”

Escalate cleanly

When: trade-offs local teams can’t make · disagreement is stuck · risk exceeds delegated authority. Bring: shared goal, options, trade-offs, recommendation, decision needed.

Traps
  • Every decision routed through Staff+ approval (“just tell me what to do”).
  • Circulating only after your mind has hardened: review as theatre.
  • Drive-by style feedback that causes churn.
  • “Strong opinions loosely held” used as a licence to dominate.
  • Disagree-and-commit used to shut debate down instead of closing it cleanly.
  • Confusing confidence with certainty; escalation used as a threat.
