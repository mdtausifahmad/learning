Leadership Skills · Reference 06

Glue Work

Compressed from Lesson 06•Print-friendly
Mindset

The problem isn’t glue work. It’s invisible allocation and invisible credit. Done deliberately, glue work is Staff+ leadership. Done unconsciously, it limits your career. Name it, scope it, measure it, rotate it.

Deliberate vs unconscious

Managed deliberatelyLeft unconscious
Named in planningAssumed to be personality or “helpfulness”
Connected to outcomesTreated as background hygiene
Distributed across the teamFalls to the same person every time
Included in promotion evidenceMissing from the performance review
Paired with technical ownershipReplaces technical ownership

Is this glue work promotable?

  • Changes how the team works — not just how one task gets done.
  • Reduces future coordination cost.
  • Leaves durable artifacts — docs, templates, dashboards, rituals, ownership maps.
  • Develops other engineers.
  • Improves an outcome — delivery, quality, reliability or decision-making.
  • Visible to the right stakeholders.

Non-promotable smell: “always” — always the note-taker, scheduler, private conflict-smoother, onboarder or ticket-cleaner, with no credit or authority.

Planning sentence (script)

I will spend one day per week for six weeks on onboarding and alignment for the payments migration; expected outcomes are two ramped engineers, fewer review bottlenecks, and a decision log that prevents repeat debates.

If it matters, it deserves capacity. If it doesn’t deserve capacity, why are you doing it? Don’t smuggle it into evenings.

Vague → scoped leadership

VagueScoped
“Helping with onboarding”“Cut ramp time for two new engineers from six weeks to four.”
“Keeping the roadmap updated”“Single source of truth for migration dependencies.”
“Asking questions”“Surfaced unresolved API ownership and security assumptions before build.”
“Keeping people aligned”“Weekly dependency sync until launch readiness was stable.”

Brag-doc bullet formula

Leadership verb + artifact or mechanism + before → after + beneficiaries.

Verbs: aligned · unblocked · reduced · clarified · delegated · accelerated · de-risked · standardised · mentored · operationalised. (Never “helped”.)

  • “Created a dependency map and weekly decision review that cut cross-team launch blockers from seven to two and let us cut scope before the freeze.”
  • “Built a ramp plan, review checklist and pairing rotation: two new engineers shipped to production independently by week four.”

Evidence to capture

Glue workEvidence
OnboardingTime to first merged PR, independent on-call, new-hire survey
Roadmap alignmentFewer priority reversals, clearer dependency dates
Design reviewRisks found before build, decisions logged
CoordinationBlockers resolved, launch readiness improved

Where to make it visible (while it happens)

PlaceUse
Planning docsCoordination named as capacity and scope
Weekly updatesRisks reduced, decisions made, people unblocked
Brag docBefore and after evidence
Promotion packetLeadership, leverage, organisational impact
RetrosInvisible coordination becomes team learning
Manager 1:1s“Does this map to level expectations?”

Rotate it

Rotatable: facilitation · notes and decision log · on-call cleanup · roadmap hygiene · onboarding buddy · postmortem follow-up · design-doc question review.

Each rotation needs: scope, time commitment, success criteria, training, rotation date, recognition mechanism.

Ask: Is this assigned because it fits this person’s growth path, or because they are reliable and no one else wants it?

Ignored-work gate (before investing heavily)

  • Tie to pain — customer pain, delivery risk, quality, reliability or talent development.
  • Find a sponsor — who will value it at review time?
  • Otherwise shrink and rotate — don’t become private unpaid infrastructure.
Traps
  • Calling all coordination “glue” without separating leadership from chores.
  • Assuming helpfulness is rewarded automatically.
  • Explaining impact only at review season (it sounds like self-justification).
  • Letting glue replace technical growth; becoming the team’s conflict sponge.
  • Junior or underrepresented engineers becoming the default sink.
  • Reporting activity (“ran 40 meetings”) rather than outcomes.
