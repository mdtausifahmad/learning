Leadership Skills · Reference 03

Project Leadership

Compressed from Lesson 03•Print-friendly
Mindset

Technical direction is part-time product management for technology. Start from the organisation’s needs, not your favourite tools. A project is done when behaviour changes, not when the code merges.

Product questions, technical version

PM questionAsk instead
Who is the customer?Which teams and users does this system constrain?
What problem matters now?What technical limitation blocks strategy or safety?
Smallest valuable slice?What migration or platform step changes behaviour soon?
How do we measure success?Which operational, delivery or quality metric moves?
What trade-off are we making?What do we defer to create leverage now?

Scoping the ambiguous (turn blur into bounded uncertainty)

  • Outcome — what are we trying to create? Why now?
  • Behaviour change — which teams must change what they do?
  • Top three risks — and which decisions are reversible?
  • Silent dependency — what could block us without anyone noticing?
  • First valuable slice — what creates learning and value earliest?
  • Stop condition — what would make us stop?

Traits that matter: perseverance (survive the first broken plan), courage (surface bad news), talking to people (find constraints before they explode). A plan is a communication tool and a hypothesis, not control.

Aggressive delegation: essential → adjacent

ElementScript
Outcome“Reduce export failure rate below 0.5%.”
Authority“You own the design review and the final recommendation.”
Guardrails“Don’t change customer-facing APIs without product sign-off.”
Support“I’ll review drafts and join escalation meetings if needed.”
Visibility“You present the update in the platform sync.”

Give away hard, growth-producing work, not leftovers. When it’s theirs, let it stay theirs: don’t re-centre yourself at the moment of success.

Cross-team alignment: both org charts

Formal org chartShadow org chart
Reporting lines, accountable leadersTrusted reviewers, incident veterans, informal architects, managers with veto, people whose silence signals risk
Ignore it → you bypass accountable leaders and breed resentmentIgnore it → you miss the person everyone waits for
  • 1. Decision-maker — identify the accountable one.
  • 2. Veto holders & influencers — name them.
  • 3. Meet in their context — before you ask for commitment.
  • 4. Write it down — decisions and assumptions.
  • 5. Explicit trade-offs — visible, even to people who disagree.
  • 6. Managers informed — without making every issue an escalation.

Technical quality: cheapest tool first

#ToolEscalate when…
1Hot spots: where defects, toil, incidents clusterFixes keep recurring
2Best practices: simple published guidanceGuidance isn’t adopted
3Leverage points: shared systems many teams touchProblems span many systems
4Technical vectors: directional betsTeams drift apart
5Measurement: actionable quality signalsSignals don’t move
6Technical-quality teamCoordination cost is too high
7Quality program: funding, governance, accountabilityPortfolio-level problem

Frame by consequence (incidents, cycle time, customer trust, onboarding, compliance, cloud cost), never by morality. A metric no team can act on is dashboard decoration.

Status update template

  • Colour + reason — “Yellow: dependency X slipped a week.”
  • Progress — since the last update.
  • Risks & decisions needed — with owners.
  • Asks — by owner and date.
  • Next milestone + confidence — high, medium or low, and why.

Definition of landed

Intended behaviour has changed · the system operates safely · owners know what remains · the organisation learned something durable.

Traps
  • Technology-centred leadership: a favourite framework in search of a problem.
  • Being the bottleneck because every hard decision routes through you.
  • Delegating only easy work and hoarding the growth opportunities.
  • Ignoring informal influencers until they become blockers.
  • Quality as a cleanup crusade rather than a managed program.
  • Green, green, green… suddenly red. Declaring victory at launch.
