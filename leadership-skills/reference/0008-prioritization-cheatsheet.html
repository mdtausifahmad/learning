Leadership Skills · Reference 08

Prioritization

Compressed from Lesson 08•Print-friendly
Mindset

Leverage is value created per unit of scarce attention, and it comes mostly from what you choose not to do. Existential issues come first. Then work where matter, room and attention overlap. Future You is a stakeholder.

Triage filter

Name the anti-pattern

Anti-patternFeels likeDangerTell
SnackingQuick wins, easy fixes, inbox zeroMotion without progressSpikes when the important work feels ambiguous
PreeningVisible, impressive-looking workVisibility mistaken for impactFlashy talk or dashboard; outcomes unchanged
Chasing ghostsHard, vague, elegant problemsEffort the org won’t valueNo sponsor, no adoption path, no plausible trigger

Is it existential?

  • Large downside — customer trust, reliability, security, strategy or talent concentration.
  • Underestimated — the organisation isn’t pricing it in yet.
  • You can move it — Staff+ attention creates clarity, options or momentum.

Existential ≠ loud. The loudest issue may be someone’s preference; the existential one may be a quiet architectural limit or a team-health problem.

Six-question filter

  • 1. Goal — is it connected to a meaningful organisational goal?
  • 2. Owner — is there a clear owner and decision forum?
  • 3. Unique — is my involvement uniquely useful?
  • 4. Durable — can it create lasting leverage?
  • 5. Cost — what will I stop doing to make room?
  • 6. Signal — how will I know it worked?

Portfolio check: impact, leverage, energy, sponsor

QuestionWhy
Impactful?Connects to outcomes
Leveraged?Multiplies beyond your direct effort
Energising enough?Sustains performance (Michelle Bu)
Sponsored?Raises the odds of adoption

If all your important work is draining, redesign the portfolio.

Saying no (trade-off, not apology)

  • “I can’t take this on this week without dropping the migration review. Which is more important?”
  • “This looks reversible; I trust the team to decide, and I can review outcomes later.”
  • “I agree it’s useful, but I don’t think it’s Staff+ leverage right now.”
  • “I can help frame the problem, but I shouldn’t be the owner.”

Time-defence policies

Office hours for advice · written intake for architecture reviews · criteria for joining meetings · delegation paths for reversible calls · deep-work blocks · a public list of priorities and non-priorities. Saying no is easier when your yes is visible.

Telegraph future constraints early. Do tidy-up work only when it adds leverage: simplify a boundary, remove a recurring incident source, clarify a de facto rule, retire a confusing process. Write institutional memory while context is fresh.

Calendar audit labels

leverage | snack | preen | ghost | necessary maintenance — one week, every block. Then write a not-doing list and share it with your manager.

Pacing: the forty-year career

Fewer, better bets · alternate intensity with recovery · build systems, not dependency · delegate growth opportunities · keep your tools sharp. A tired Staff+ engineer compensates with control: more reviews, more meetings, more direct decisions.

Traps
  • Mistaking busyness for impact.
  • Saying yes to easy work because the strategic work feels ambiguous.
  • Choosing visible work because promotion systems notice it.
  • Fighting ignored problems with no sponsor or adoption path.
  • Treating every urgent request as important; a calendar designed by other people’s anxiety.
  • Burning out and calling it commitment.
