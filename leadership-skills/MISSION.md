# Mission: Leadership Skills (Part B), Senior → Staff/Principal

## Why
A technically strong senior engineer wants to reach **Staff or Principal**. The jump is not a technical one. At Staff+, authority *shrinks*: you lead through **influence, communication and force-multiplication**, not command. Promotion committees look for evidence that your impact travels through other people and teams. That evidence comes from leadership practice that is deliberate, visible and repeatable. Knowing the vocabulary is not enough.

This course is Part B of [the skills map](../02-skills-to-upgrade.md). Its output feeds straight into the [promotion playbook](../05-promotion-playbook.md) (packet, STAR-E stories, sponsor) and the [self-assessment tracker](../06-self-assessment-and-tracking.md) (brag doc, cadence). Rule of thumb: the promotion is usually won on **influence, communication and sponsorship**.

## Success looks like
- **Influence without authority:** change decisions in teams you don't manage. Use the three moves (questions, purpose, room). Handle difficult people before the meeting. Follow quickly when a fight doesn't matter, and mark feedback as non-blocking.
- **Executive communication:** open with SCQA, structure with the Minto Pyramid and pre-align through nemawashi. Write a one-page memo that leads with the answer and names the ask.
- **Technical & project leadership:** scope an ambiguous multi-team initiative and delegate hard work with guardrails. Navigate the shadow org chart, run quality as a program, and land behaviour change, not just merged code.
- **Mentorship & sponsorship:** spend credibility to create opportunity for others, using specific, evidence-grade praise and a sponsorship journal. Create space and don't take the work back.
- **Stakeholders & trust:** never surprise your manager and don't let your sponsor surprise you. Manage political capital as a bank account and build a peer network before you need it.
- **Glue work:** do it deliberately. Scope it in planning, rotate it and turn it into brag-doc evidence.
- **Decision-making:** transfer judgement through written reasoning. Separate style from substance and decision quality from outcome. Pick the lightest framework (DACI, one-way/two-way door, disagree and commit).
- **Prioritization:** invest in existential issues first, then where matter, room and attention overlap. Refuse snacking, preening and chasing ghosts. Pace yourself for a forty-year career.
- **Proof:** a 90-day leadership plan, executed, that produces promotion-packet evidence for at least four of the eight skills.

## Constraints
- About **2–4 hrs/week** of reading, plus practice that is **embedded in real work**: design reviews, planning, 1:1s and incidents. Every lesson ends with try-it actions for *this week*.
- No direct reports are assumed. Everything works through borrowed authority.
- Company-agnostic: map the ideas to your own ladder and calibration process.
