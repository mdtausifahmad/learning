Technical Skills · Reference 08

T-shape Development Cheat Sheet

Compressed from Lesson 08•Print-friendly
Mindset

Not “learn everything” but learn the right adjacent things while deepening the authority the business needs. Breadth earns you a seat in the room; depth gives your judgment weight.

Shapes of expertise

ShapeStrengthRisk
Pure specialistDeep authority in a nicheFragile if the niche fades
Pure generalistNavigates many domainsToo shallow to set direction
T-shapedBreadth + one spikeOverloaded as the bridge for everything
Pi-shapedBreadth + two spikes (common over a long career)Needs deliberate focus to avoid dilution

Breadth = working fluency, not trivia

DomainMinimum useful fluency
FrontendRendering model, performance, accessibility, client state
BackendAPI boundaries, concurrency, failure handling, deployment
DataModelling, indexing, transactions, retention, analytics
InfrastructureCompute, networking, scaling, IAM, cost
SecurityAuthN, AuthZ, threat modelling, secure defaults
OperationsObservability, SLOs, incident response, capacity
ProductUser workflows, business model, customer pain

Breadth lets you ask cross-domain questions, understand specialist language, spot missing stakeholders, connect incidents to design, and weigh trade-offs. It compounds: each domain adds hooks that speed up learning the next.

Choosing your spike

  • Energising to you and enduringly central to the business — years, not a quarter.
  • Where expensive decisions repeat, or incidents and bottlenecks cluster.
  • Where differentiation depends on technical excellence.
  • Where no clear authority exists yet.
  • Not because it is fashionable — an unneeded specialty is strategically limiting.
Company contextPlausible spike
High-scale consumerReliability, latency, experimentation platform
Fintech / regulatedSecurity, privacy, ledger correctness, compliance architecture
Data-heavy SaaSAnalytics infra, data modelling, search, ML platform
Developer toolsAPI design, extensibility, build systems
MarketplaceRanking, payments, fraud, event-driven systems

Build breadth through real exposure

  • Rotate or take temporary assignments in adjacent domains.
  • Read real artifacts — architecture docs, RFCs, incident reviews.
  • Shadow on-call for systems you depend on; join design reviews outside your area.
  • Pair with specialists during migrations, outages, performance work; prototype unfamiliar tools.
  • Ask in every design: “what breaks at 10×?” and “who operates this?”

Build depth through ownership under pressure

  • Own a critical domain for multiple years.
  • Read source, design docs, postmortems and historical decisions.
  • Lead root-cause analysis and follow systemic fixes through.
  • Benchmark and measure instead of trusting folklore.
  • Teach and encode — docs, talks, office hours, tools, standards, ADRs. Expertise that lives only in your head doesn’t scale.

Archetypes need both bars

ArchetypeBreadth needDepth need
Tech LeadProduct, team, architecture, delivery trade-offsEnough to make credible technical calls
ArchitectSystem-wide interactions, future constraintsDeep authority in core domains
SolverWhere the hardest problem sits across layersVery deep debugging and problem solving
Right HandTranslate executive priorities across domainsDeep trust and judgment in critical systems

Quarterly loop

1 Pick one breadth domain → 2 pick one depth capability → 3 find real work that forces learning → 4 produce a scaling artifact → 5 ask specialists to critique you.

GoalReal workScaling artifact
Security breadthThreat-model a new APISecurity review checklist
Database depthLead a high-write migrationMigration playbook + ADR
Cloud breadthJoin a cost-reduction effortCost review guide
Observability depthRedesign service SLOsSLO template + dashboard standard
Traps
  • Breadth as shallow awareness instead of working fluency.
  • Becoming the only bridge across teams — a bottleneck.
  • Staying in a comfortable niche after the business moved on.
  • Confusing years of experience with depth.
  • Reading broadly without doing real cross-domain work; private, unteachable expertise.
