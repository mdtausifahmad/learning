Technical Skills · Reference 02

Reliability & SRE Cheat Sheet

Compressed from Lesson 02•Print-friendly
Mindset

Reliability is managed measurably, not heroically: a user-centred SLO plus an agreed error-budget policy decides when to ship and when to stabilise.

SLI → SLO → budget → policy

TermDefinitionExample
SLIMeasured signal approximating user experience% of valid checkout requests completing < 800 ms
SLOTarget for an SLI over a window99.9% of checkouts succeed over 30 days
SLAExternal promise with consequencesCredits if monthly availability < 99.5%
Error budget1 − SLO over the window0.1% of requests may fail in 30 days

Budget numbers to remember (30-day window, if failures were full outages)

SLOBudgetSLOBudget

What to measure

LensSignalsUse for
Golden signalsLatency, Traffic, Errors, SaturationBaseline dashboard for user-facing services
REDRate, Errors, DurationRequest-driven services
USEUtilization, Saturation, ErrorsResources (CPU, pools, disks, queues)
Three pillarsMetrics, logs, tracesTrends & alerts / case detail / cross-service paths
  • Stable names and controlled cardinality for metrics.
  • Correlation IDs through every hop; a deliberate sampling strategy.
  • Owners and retention defined per signal; alert on burn rate and user impact, not every anomaly.
  • Percentiles, not means — users live in the tail.

Toil test

Toil is manual, repetitive, automatable, tactical, scales linearly with load, and leaves no enduring value. Novel incident debugging and SLO design are engineering, not toil. Cap toil with a toil budget; when it is exceeded, invest in automation, self-healing or redesign — but never automate a bad process.

Incident response

RoleOwns
Incident CommanderCoordination, priorities, decisions, timeline
Operations LeadTechnical investigation and mitigation
Communications LeadStakeholders, status page, support, executives
ScribeTimeline, decisions, commands, hypotheses

Flow: alert → triage severity (by user/business impact) → assign roles → mitigate first → communicate → resolve → postmortem → track and verify follow-ups.

Blameless postmortem checklist

  • Impact — who, how badly, how long. Detection — how found, how late.
  • Timeline of events, decisions and signals.
  • Contributing factors — technical, organisational, process, knowledge.
  • What went well / poorly — which defences held, failed or were missing.
  • Action items that change defaults, automate checks, shrink blast radius or surface failure sooner — owned, prioritised, verified.

Overload & cascading failure

PatternPurposeWatch out for
Load sheddingReject excess work before collapseShed low-value work first; return clear errors
Graceful degradationKeep critical journeys aliveProduct decisions needed before the incident
Backoff + jitterSpread and desynchronise retriesNon-idempotent retries duplicate effects
Circuit breakerStop calling a failing dependencyBad thresholds oscillate or hide recovery
BulkheadIsolate by tenant, feature, dependencyOver-partitioning wastes capacity

Thundering herd: many clients retry, reconnect or refill caches at once → jitter, request coalescing, token buckets, stampede control.

Safe change

  • Deploy dark → canary → expand → full, gated on SLIs.
  • Feature flags separate deploy from release — and get removed afterwards.
  • Rollback is tested and compatible with schema changes and mixed versions.
  • NALSD — real numbers, failure domains, owners, rollout mechanics; a design that cannot say how it fails is unfinished.
Traps
  • SLOs on host uptime or CPU instead of user journeys.
  • SLOs so strict they’re ignored, or so loose users suffer.
  • Retrying everything without timeouts, budgets, idempotency or jitter.
  • Action items that say “be careful” or “add monitoring”.
  • Permanent, undocumented feature flags.
  • Expecting on-call heroics to cover unclear ownership or excess complexity.
